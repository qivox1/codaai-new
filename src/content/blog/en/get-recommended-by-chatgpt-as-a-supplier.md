Ask ChatGPT which suppliers are worth considering for a particular job and you do not get a list of results but three to five names. Only those companies make the buyer's longlist. **ChatGPT names the companies it either knows from training or finds confirmed in several independent sources during a web search.** Both can be influenced, but not with the tools that were enough for Google.

In our [study of 449 mid-sized companies in Germany](/en/study/), **54.7% of 3,592 buyer questions went without a mention** of the company examined. For 46.8% of the companies, ChatGPT and Google AI Overviews recommended competitors by name instead.

## How does ChatGPT decide which suppliers to name?

ChatGPT decides in two steps: first it assembles a list of candidates, then it picks the names for which it has the most consistent evidence. The candidates come from two sources.

The first is [model knowledge](/en/knowledge/geo-glossary/model-knowledge/) – everything the model read during training. Companies described often and consistently there belong to its fixed repertoire. Companies that have renamed, repositioned or spun off since the training cut-off are missing.

The second is [web search](/en/knowledge/geo-glossary/web-search/). ChatGPT triggers it when it is not sure of the answer. The question is split into several sub-questions, pages are fetched for each, and only individual passages make it into the answer. For web search, the Bing index matters most: in an analysis by Seer Interactive, 87% of SearchGPT citations matched Bing's organic top results, but only 56% matched Google's ([more in the article on Bing](/en/blog/chatgpt-seo-perplexity-visibility/)).

## Why is a good Google ranking not enough?

A good Google ranking is not enough because ChatGPT often does not search at all and, when it does, uses a different index. The study shows this clearly: **34.9% of the 361 companies with more than 100 top-10 rankings on Google are completely invisible in ChatGPT.** We call this the SEO paradox.

An example from the survey: a mechanical engineering firm for factory automation with around 640 top-10 rankings was not named in any of its eight buyer questions. The most frequently named competitor appeared seven times. The company does not lack relevance; it lacks mentions on other sites, which the AI uses to build its candidate list.

On top of that, the systems differ. The same questions produced an average visibility of 24.9% in ChatGPT and 39.4% in Google AI Overviews. 39.6% of companies were never named by ChatGPT. We deliberately queried ChatGPT without web search – the figure measures what the model knows on its own.

## Which measures demonstrably work?

Five measures demonstrably work, and they build on each other. Sorted in the order in which they should be tackled:

### 1. Make your own pages readable for AI crawlers

Readability is the precondition for everything else. **No crawler from OpenAI, Anthropic, Perplexity, Meta or ByteDance executes JavaScript** ([Vercel and MERJ, December 2024](https://vercel.com/blog/the-rise-of-the-ai-crawler)). Service descriptions that only come into being in the browser or only sit in PDFs do not exist for these systems. That also means not locking out the [AI crawlers](/en/knowledge/geo-glossary/llm-crawlers/) in robots.txt.

### 2. Describe the company the same way everywhere

Consistency is what AI checks when it weighs the evidence. Among the 1,000 most-cited pages in ChatGPT, 23.8% are home and landing pages ([Ahrefs, October 2025](https://ahrefs.com/blog/chatgpts-most-cited-pages/)) – your own home page is one of the few sources a company fully controls. Company name, category and service description should read the same there, in directories and on profiles. More in the glossary under [consistent brand description](/en/knowledge/geo-glossary/consistent-brand-description/).

### 3. Get mentioned on third-party sites

Mentions on other sites are the strongest single factor measured. Ahrefs found for 75,000 brands that [brand mentions on the web](/en/knowledge/geo-glossary/brand-mentions/) correlate with being named in Google AI Overviews at **0.664**, backlinks only at **0.218** ([Ahrefs, 2025](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overview-brand-correlation/)). What counts is the mention in the right subject context, not the link. Correlation is not causation, and the sample consists of large brands.

### 4. Appear in your industry's comparison lists

Comparison lists are the source cited most often in purchase decisions. In an analysis of around 1,260 B2B purchase prompts, **70.8% of all citations** pointed to pages with “best”, “top” or “leading” in the title ([Overthink Group, July 2026](https://overthinkgroup.com/b2b-ai-citation-stats-2026-q2/)). The data comes from the US software market, but the direction matches what we observe in German B2B.

### 5. Write content that can be cited

Citable content decides whether your own page is linked as a source. The Princeton study on Generative Engine Optimization (Aggarwal et al., KDD 2024) tested nine text measures on 10,000 queries. The strongest were verbatim quotations (+41%), statistics (+34%) and source references (+28%) – exactly what a model cannot generate itself. Keyword stuffing brought no gain. One format that is often overlooked is [expert videos on YouTube](/en/blog/youtube-ai-visibility-b2b/).

## What does not work?

Not every measure sold as GEO has evidence behind it. We explicitly advise against four of them:

- **llms.txt as a lever.** 97% of the files examined were never fetched by an AI crawler ([Ahrefs, 137,210 domains](https://ahrefs.com/blog/llmstxt-study/)). The file does no harm, but it does not replace any of the measures above.
- **Placement guarantees.** Nobody can promise a mention in ChatGPT, because nobody controls the answers.
- **Planting mentions in forums.** In niche B2B purchase prompts, Reddit accounted for 1.4% of citations ([Overthink Group](https://overthinkgroup.com/b2b-ai-citation-stats-2026-q2/)). On top of that comes the risk of being spotted as self-promotion.
- **Stuffing texts with search terms.** In the Princeton study this brought no gain, and on Perplexity it even caused a loss.

The full list with evidence is on the services page under [“Six things we deliberately do not offer”](/en/digital-visibility/#not-included).

## How can you check whether ChatGPT names your company?

A mention can only be checked as a share across many runs, not with a single question. AI answers vary widely: in a SparkToro study with 2,961 runs, the same list of suppliers appeared twice in fewer than one in a hundred cases, the same order in fewer than one in a thousand ([Rand Fishkin, January 2026](https://sparktoro.com/blog/new-research-ais-are-highly-inconsistent-when-recommending-brands-or-products-marketers-should-take-care-when-tracking-ai-visibility/)). A screenshot therefore proves neither that you are named nor that you are missing.

What holds up is a fixed [prompt set](/en/knowledge/geo-glossary/prompt-set/): eight to twelve questions your buyers really ask, put to ChatGPT and Google AI Overviews several times. That gives you the [mention rate](/en/knowledge/geo-glossary/mention-rate/) – the share of answers in which you appear – and the list of competitors named instead. Recording this before the first measure gives you a [baseline measurement](/en/knowledge/geo-glossary/baseline-measurement/), so you can prove progress later. What such a measurement looks like in practice is shown in our [analysis of 7,184 AI answers](/en/blog/ai-visibility-chatgpt-recommendation-practice/).

## Where should you start?

Start with an honest assessment of where you stand, not with a measure. The order that follows from the evidence:

1. **Measure.** Put five buyer questions to ChatGPT and Google AI Overviews, three times each, and note who is named.
2. **Become readable.** Check whether your most important service pages are readable without JavaScript and whether AI crawlers are allowed.
3. **Become consistent.** Align company name, category and service description on your home page, profiles and directories.
4. **Get mentioned.** Find the sources AI cites for your questions and be present there with substance – in trade media, comparison lists and industry directories.

The order is no accident: mentions on third-party sites only work if AI finds them confirmed on your own website. Starting with step four means building without a foundation.
