GEO (Generative Engine Optimization) is the work of getting an AI system to mention a company in its answer or to cite its content as a source. This means systems such as ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, Microsoft Copilot and Google AI Overviews. The term sets itself apart from SEO: SEO wants a position in a results list, GEO wants a role in a generated answer.

## How does GEO work?

An AI answer draws on two sources of knowledge. The first is [model knowledge](/en/knowledge/geo-glossary/model-knowledge/), meaning what the language model saw during training. The second is web search, which the system triggers when it is not confident in its answer; the results flow into the answer via [grounding](/en/knowledge/geo-glossary/grounding/). GEO works on both paths: content must be available in a form that crawlers can capture, it must rank for the relevant search queries, individual passages must hold up in [re-ranking](/en/knowledge/geo-glossary/re-ranking/), and the brand must be recognisable as an entity via third-party sites.

A workable formula for this: **AI visibility = (technology + content + off-page) × processes.** Technology gets a page into the race, content decides whether it is cited, the off-page environment decides whether the brand counts as a candidate at all, and without measurement processes every measure remains a one-off action.

## Why does GEO matter for companies?

Anyone looking for a service today increasingly asks an AI first. It usually names a few providers, and whoever is not among them does not feature in the decision. In the CodaAI study "AI Blind Test 2026", which evaluates 7,184 AI answers about 449 mid-sized B2B companies in Germany, the name of the company under review does not come up in 55% of buyer questions; in 47% of questions the AI recommends competitors instead. Traffic on your own website shows none of this, because the decision is made before the click.

## What does GEO mean for your website?

GEO is not a performance marketing channel; it is closer to brand building: it is about presence and a consistent picture in answers, not primarily about clicks. Three questions structure the work: Can the AI read your content (technology)? Are you where the AI gets its evidence (off-page)? Does it cite you because your content answers a question better than others (content)? Measurement is not done with a position but with [mention rate](/en/knowledge/geo-glossary/mention-rate/), [citation rate](/en/knowledge/geo-glossary/citation-rate/) and [share of AI search](/en/knowledge/geo-glossary/share-of-ai-search/) across a fixed [prompt set](/en/knowledge/geo-glossary/prompt-set/).
