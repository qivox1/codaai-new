A consistent brand description exists when a brand is described with the same wording everywhere on the web: name, core service, target group, location. Not only on its own website, but in company profiles, review platforms, trade media, social networks and knowledge bases — and by third parties just as by the brand itself. For AI systems, this consistency is the prerequisite for recognising a brand as a clear [entity](/en/knowledge/geo-glossary/entity/).

## How does a consistent brand description work?

A language model forms its picture of a brand from all the sources it has read. If ten sources describe a brand the same way, a stable link emerges: this brand stands for this service in this region. If the sources describe it differently — the website speaks of "Digital Visibility", the industry directory of "content agency", an old press release of "AI platform" — the picture stays blurred, and the model does not reliably mention the brand for any of the three services. The AI system does not care whether the information comes from the brand itself or from third parties; what counts is agreement.

## Why does a consistent brand description matter for AI visibility?

Because it decides on mention and correctness. A blurred entity is mentioned less often. And when it is mentioned, the model adopts the version that occurs most frequently — in case of contradictions, possibly the outdated one. Brand-specific questions in the [prompt set](/en/knowledge/geo-glossary/prompt-set/) show exactly this: does the LLM have all the correct information about my brand?

## What does this mean for your website?

Define a short description — one sentence for the service, one sentence for the target group, fixed spellings for names and products — and enter it everywhere your brand is described: website, legal notice, Google Business Profile, LinkedIn, [review platforms](/en/knowledge/geo-glossary/review-platforms/), industry directories, Wikidata, press releases. Check old descriptions that still carry an earlier positioning, and correct them at the source.
