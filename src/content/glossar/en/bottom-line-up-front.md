Bottom Line Up Front (BLUF) is a writing principle that puts the conclusion first. Instead of leading up to a statement, a text begins with it: the direct answer or a summary sits in the first sentences, the reasoning follows. For AI search, the rule of thumb is that the key insights should sit in the first 30% of a page.

## How does Bottom Line Up Front work?

The pattern repeats at every level. The page begins with the core statement. Each section begins with the answer to the question in its heading. Each paragraph carries its statement in the first sentence. Evidence, examples and caveats come afterwards. Whoever reads only the first sentence, a person skimming or a system extracting, still knows the statement.

The opposite is the classic build-up pattern: introduction, problem, background, solution, conclusion. It reads pleasantly but pushes the actual answer to the back part of the page.

## Why does Bottom Line Up Front matter for AI visibility?

AI systems extract passages that answer a question ([grounding snippets](/en/knowledge/geo-glossary/grounding-snippets/)), and they give a page only limited room in the context. An answer that sits in the second-to-last paragraph competes with an answer that sits in the first paragraph of another page, and loses. BLUF ensures that the extractable part of the page carries the statement instead of an introduction.

## What does this mean for your website?

Check your most important pages with one question: if someone reads only the first three sentences, do they know the answer? If not, move the statement up. Build a short conclusion before the footer, but do not save the most important insight for it. Combine BLUF with [entity echoing](/en/knowledge/geo-glossary/entity-echoing/): the first sentence begins with the term the heading asks about.
