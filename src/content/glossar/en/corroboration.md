Corroboration is the confirmation of a statement by several independent sources. In AI search, the term describes a trust signal: if many sources outside your website say the same thing about your company, an AI system is more likely to include that statement in its answer. SEO consultant Lily Grozeva calls corroboration “the more complex word for trust” (LLM Mastery, August 2026).

## How does corroboration work?

Grozeva describes corroboration as the reasons an AI system has to trust a brand: an office, a phone number, a visible team, membership of industry associations, involvement in the community, presence at events. From around 50 B2B audits she concludes that the classic ranking factors are not what decides. Three other things are: clarity of the entity, corroboration from the ecosystem outside your own website, and consistency of representation.

Corroboration takes effect in three places:

- **During grounding:** according to Mike King (iPullRank), AI systems look for information that is consistent across several sources. Based on his reading of Google patents, [AI Overviews](/en/knowledge/geo-glossary/ai-overviews/) generate an answer first and then look for documents that support it.
- **During training:** a language model learns from large volumes of text. What survives, according to Duane Forrester, is consensus, not code (July 2026).
- **In the knowledge graph:** according to Kalicube, knowledge graphs take in content partly to confirm existing facts. The [entity home](/en/knowledge/geo-glossary/entity-home/) serves as the reconciliation point.

Tool vendors reach the same conclusion. Ahrefs names consensus, alongside freshness and authority, as one of three factors behind the likelihood of being cited (August 2026). Malte Landwehr (Peec AI) says that AI systems searching the web want consensus from several sources (August 2026).

## How does corroboration differ from consistency?

Aleyda Solís lists the two separately in her model of ten traits shared by brands that succeed in AI search (brightonSEO, April 2026). “Consistent” means the entity signals match across the website, Wikipedia or Wikidata, LinkedIn, review sites and the press. “Corroborated” means several independent sources confirm the statements.

The difference is the sender. A [consistent brand description](/en/knowledge/geo-glossary/consistent-brand-description/) is in your hands. Corroboration comes from others — through [brand mentions](/en/knowledge/geo-glossary/brand-mentions/), reviews, reports and lists. Without consistency, there is nothing for third parties to confirm in a uniform way.

## Why does corroboration matter for AI visibility?

Corroboration often decides whether an AI system merely knows a brand or actually recommends it. Solís illustrates this with an example: a software brand appeared in 58% of answers to general discovery prompts but in only 11% of answers to shortlist prompts. The cause was a lack of external confirmation. The brand was missing from the sources cited for selection prompts. The recommended fix was placements in software round-ups and on the category pages of G2 and Capterra.

Without corroboration, other sources fill the gap. Grozeva observes that language models output outdated details about owners or executives — for example people who left the company 15 years ago. Liam Dunne points out that a model may not recommend a company at all if other sources contradict what it says about itself (May 2026).

## Where are the limits of corroboration?

Consensus on niche questions can be influenced — in both directions. In September 2026, Edward Sturm showed how a few press releases led Google to name him as the answer to a niche question. Shortly afterwards, competitors filled the results with press releases of their own. A bought consensus therefore lasts only until someone else buys more.

Koray Tuğberk Gübür (Holistic SEO) stresses that a consensus has to be renewed regularly to stay current (August 2026). Confirmation that holds up therefore comes from genuine business activity: client reviews, expert articles, association memberships, references.

## What does this mean for your website?

Corroboration starts on your own website and ends outside it:

1. **Set the facts:** state on your entity home what should be true about your company — name, services, audience, founding year.
2. **Be consistent yourself:** describe yourself the same way on every channel you own, from LinkedIn to press releases.
3. **Build confirmation:** make sure independent sources state the same facts — [review platforms](/en/knowledge/geo-glossary/review-platforms/), trade media through [digital PR](/en/knowledge/geo-glossary/digital-pr/), industry associations, comparison lists.
4. **Find the gaps:** use a [source analysis](/en/knowledge/geo-glossary/source-analysis/) to see which sources AI systems cite for your most important questions and where your company is missing from them.

Not every source counts equally. What matters are the sources AI systems actually draw on in your sector. For a machinery manufacturer they differ from those for a software vendor.
