YouTube presence is the visibility of a brand through its own videos on YouTube. For AI systems, YouTube is a source in its own right: Google embeds videos in AI Overviews and AI Mode, and ChatGPT cites YouTube links for explanation and how-to questions. In correlation analyses — such as the one by Ahrefs across 75,000 brands — YouTube presence is among the strongest factors for being mentioned in AI answers.

## How does YouTube presence affect AI answers?

AI systems read videos through title, description, chapters and, above all, the transcript. A specialist video that answers a specific question therefore delivers the same as a good text passage: an extractable chunk with answer, terms and brand name. SISTRIX has shown for the German market that a considerable share of AI Mode answers contains a YouTube link; OtterlyAI has found that many cited videos have fewer than 1,000 views — reach is not the criterion, relevance is. The blog article [YouTube and AI visibility in B2B](/en/blog/youtube-ai-visibility-b2b/) puts these figures in context, including the counter-figures: across all AI citations, YouTube is a small share.

## Why does YouTube presence matter for AI visibility?

Because it opens up a source that many competitors among mid-sized B2B companies do not serve — and because a video with the company name in the transcript is a [brand mention](/en/knowledge/geo-glossary/brand-mentions/) on a platform that AI systems weight heavily. Added to this is the [E-E-A-T](/en/knowledge/geo-glossary/e-e-a-t/) signal: a visible person explaining a specialist question is evidence of first-hand experience.

## What does this mean for your website?

Produce long-form specialist videos on the questions in your [prompt set](/en/knowledge/geo-glossary/prompt-set/) — explanations, comparisons, project insights — with the question in the title and the answer in the first minutes. Mention your brand in the spoken text and in the description. Embed the videos on the matching pages of your website, with the transcript in the HTML. Short clips have a different job: reach where nobody reads long texts, and the way back to the specialist article.
