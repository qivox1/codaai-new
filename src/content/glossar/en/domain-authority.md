Domain Authority is the weight a domain carries in its field; in the GEO context, the question is whether it counts as a leading voice. Unlike the metric of the same name in individual SEO tools, this does not mean a score but the sum of external signals: backlinks, mentions in recognised publications, entries in knowledge sources, reviews. It is the authority that others attribute to a domain.

## How does Domain Authority work in AI search?

AI systems adopt the rankings of the search engines, into which Domain Authority classically feeds. Beyond that, the signals act directly: during training, a language model has read who is cited in trade media, who is listed in Wikipedia, who is named in comparisons, and it treats these domains as more reliable sources. Analyses such as the one by Growth Memo on how AI systems weight links show that nofollow links also contribute to citations, because the context of the mention counts, not the link attribute.

Domain Authority complements [Topical Authority](/en/knowledge/geo-glossary/topical-authority/): one comes from outside and rates the domain as a whole, the other comes from inside and rates the depth on a topic.

## Why does Domain Authority matter for AI visibility?

Because, given passages of equal quality, AI systems choose the more trustworthy source, and trust comes from outside. A domain nobody cites has to convince anew with every passage. A domain that counts as a reference is preferred when in doubt. This is the third tier of visibility: being cited because others cite you.

## What does this mean for your website?

Strengthen your backlink profile, including nofollow links where the context is right. Be present in trade publications ([Digital PR](/en/knowledge/geo-glossary/digital-pr/)). Check Wikipedia and Wikidata: an entry in knowledge sources pays off because AI models train on them. And work the [review platforms](/en/knowledge/geo-glossary/review-platforms/) that are cited in your field.
